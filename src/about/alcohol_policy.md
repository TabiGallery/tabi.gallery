# Tabi Gallery Drug and Alcohol Policy

## Overview:

Tabi is a mostly sober space, with exceptions for alcohol at private events that go through approvals.

## Alcohol:

In the spirit of safety and inclusivity, Tabi is considered a ‘dry zone’, with alcohol restricted within a two meter radius of the building and is not permitted within Tabi’s shared spaces. Studio spaces are considered private spaces, and the storage of alcohol in them is not governed by this policy, however Committee Members are still expected to be considerate of how their behavior reflects on Tabi and best represent our policies, even in private spaces.

Public spaces in Tabi may be booked for a private function, including the use of alcohol through written resolution from a committee member. The exception to this is the sensory room in which no alcohol can be stored or consumed within. Visible intoxication is not permitted at Tabi. A Committee Member can request you leave a public space if they believe you are showing signs of intoxication.

The storage of alcohol is not permitted in Tabi’s shared spaces. If alcohol is brought into Tabi, a Committee Member can request your alcohol be removed or more discreetly stored.

Under no circumstances can alcohol be consumed in a public space. Breach of this policy will result in the offender being requested to leave the premises or retreat to a private space.

## Drugs

Tabi is an illicit drug free zone at all times. It is against Tabi policy to use, possess or be intoxicated by illicit drugs on the premises and within 2 meters of the building in which Tabi resides.

The use of Tobacco or E-Cigarettes are prohibited within 2 meters of Tabi

The use and storage of prescribed drugs are permitted as long as they are stored correctly and in the issued container.

If someone’s behaviour is indicative of substance use, a Committee Member or responsible person should be sensitively informed so they can take appropriate action. A Committee Member has the right to request you to leave the premises if it is believed our drug and intoxication policy has been breached.

Tabi recognises that a range of conditions or disabilities exist that may cause a person to display symptoms or signs similar to those of intoxication and we commit to being considerate and mindful of this when assessing a suspicion of drug intoxication.

Police will not be called except in circumstances where all community-based options have been exhausted and there is an immediate threat to safety. We recognise police presence often escalates harm, especially for marginalised people.
